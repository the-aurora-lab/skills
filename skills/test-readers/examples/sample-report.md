## Test read: grant-summary.md (Fresh Air Classrooms)

Five readers took part: a governor (Newcomer), an air-quality scientist (Expert), a finance officer (Decision-maker), a head teacher (practitioner) and an editor. Each was a separate fresh-context subagent that saw only the draft. 19 October 2026.
Scope: a near-final draft, so this covers clarity, accuracy and language at line level. The yardstick is the message you gave me: cheap CO2 monitors with a simple alert can keep classroom air fresh without new ventilation equipment, and the project needs £74,500 over 18 months to test this in 12 schools.

**Overall.** Every reader understood the idea and liked it. All five said they would not fund this draft. Three problems cost the most trust:
- The pilot is said to have "proved" a 20% fall in absences.
- The budget lines add up to **£72,500**, but the stated total is **£74,500**. I checked this sum myself.
- The draft says 12 schools in one place and 10 in another.

Each reader spotted at least two of these, and most spotted all three. After that they read the rest as a sales pitch. All three are quick to fix, and the case underneath them is strong.

**Keep.**
- **The Background paragraph.** "A thousand hours a year" and "windows stay shut to keep the heat in" are concrete details every reader recognised.
- **The mechanism.** The light turns amber and the teacher opens a window for five minutes. Every reader could picture it without any technical knowledge.
- **The cost comparison in "Why it matters".** "Tens of thousands per building vs under £100" was the line that made the finance officer, the governor and the head teacher *want* to fund it.
- **The teacher anecdote.** Children reminding their teacher when the light changed rang true, especially for the head teacher. It works as colour. It doesn't work as proof of scale.
- **Choosing both Victorian and post-war system builds.** The scientist called this "the most scientifically thoughtful line".
- **The structure.** The document is short, has clear headings, and the budget is itemised.

The core message landed with every reader. Two things drifted from it:
- **The air-quality message was crowded out.** Readers came away with absence reduction as the headline claim, and in a form they distrusted. The "keep classroom air fresh" message was overshadowed.
- **The cost message drifted.** "Under £100 a monitor" reached all of them, but the finance officer and head teacher then worked out that the real cost is about £1,550 per classroom (about £6,200 per school). That gap undercut the pitch.
